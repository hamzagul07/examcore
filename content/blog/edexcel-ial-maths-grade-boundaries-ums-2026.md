Edexcel International A Level (IAL) Mathematics does **not** use Cambridge-style raw syllabus thresholds. Units such as Pure Mathematics 1 (**WMA11**) earn a **raw mark**, which converts to a **uniform mark (UMS)** for that unit; your overall grade is built by **cashing in** the UMS totals you need for the qualification. If you paste a Cambridge 9709 threshold PDF onto an IAL unit score, you will misread your position.

## Raw mark vs UMS vs cash-in

1. **Raw mark** — what the examiner awards on that sitting of that unit (method + accuracy, with follow-through where the scheme allows).  
2. **UMS** — the scaled mark for the unit after grade boundaries for that paper/session are applied. Harder papers typically need fewer raw marks for the same UMS band.  
3. **Cash-in** — the combination of unit UMS scores that produces your overall qualification grade.

Think of UMS as the common currency. Your job while revising is still **maximising raw marks with clean method**; UMS is how Pearson makes those marks comparable when units are mixed across sessions.

For the Cambridge cousin of this idea (Percentage Uniform Mark), see [PUM explained](/blog/cambridge-pum-percentage-uniform-marks-explained-2026) — same intuition, different board rules. Do not mix the two on one statement.

## Why Cambridge threshold posts mislead IAL students

Cambridge International publishes **grade threshold tables** per syllabus code (for example 9709) after each series. Those tables answer: “What raw total / option code earned an A this session?”

Edexcel IAL Maths answers a different question: “What raw mark on **this unit paper** maps to which UMS, and do my cashed-in units meet the overall grade?”

So:

- A Cambridge 9709 A boundary does **not** tell you whether your WMA12 raw mark is “safe.”  
- An IAL unit UMS of 80 is not the same object as a Cambridge PUM of 80 on a different qualification.

## How to use boundaries without panicking

1. **After a mock or past paper** — mark strictly against the Edexcel scheme dialect (method lines, accuracy, follow-through). Guessing a UMS from an unmarked script is fiction.  
2. **Compare to the same unit’s recent series** — not to a different unit and not to Cambridge 9709.  
3. **Plan cash-in early** — know which units your centre enters and when retakes are allowed; one weak unit can drag an otherwise strong profile.  
4. **Resit the unit, not the rumour** — if UMS is short, rebuild raw marks with timed papers and second-pass marking, not grade-boundary folklore.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is UMS the same as a percentage?

No. UMS is anchored to grade standards for that unit/session. A raw 70% on a hard paper can map differently from a raw 70% on an easier one.

### Can I cash in AS and complete A Level later?

IAL routes are modular by design; centres enter the cash-in that matches your qualification path. Confirm entries with your exams officer — do not assume Cambridge AS/A2 rules.

### Does MarkScheme store official UMS tables?

We explain the model and route you to practise marking. Official numbers come from Pearson / your centre. When we add verified tools later, they will be labelled as such — never as pre-exam predictions.

### Should I remark if I am one UMS short?

Only with centre advice and a realistic read of the script. Remarks can go down. Often the higher EV move is a planned unit resit with marked practice.
