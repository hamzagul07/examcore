International schools often choose between **Cambridge International A-Level** and **Pearson Edexcel International A Level (IAL)**. Both are serious university-entry routes. The useful question is not which logo is “better,” but whether you want **linear end-of-course papers** (Cambridge) or **modular units cashed in with UMS** (Edexcel IAL) — and whether your revision matches that exam game.

## Quick comparison

| | Cambridge International A-Level | Edexcel International A Level |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Structure | Mostly linear (papers at the end of the course) | Modular units; overall grade via UMS |
| Maths shape | Syllabus codes (e.g. 9709) with paper components | Units such as Pure 1–4, Mechanics, Statistics (WMA/WME/WST) |
| Marking flavour | Method / accuracy / independent marks (B/M/A style) | Method and accuracy marks with follow-through |
| Footprint | Very wide in international schools | Growing; common where Pearson is already used |
| MarkScheme today | Full courses + past-paper marking | IAL Maths practice marking live; shell for Physics/Chem/Bio |

Neither is a shortcut. Universities treat both as A-Level-standard international qualifications when grades match the offer.

## Modular vs linear — why it changes how you study

On **Edexcel IAL**, you can sit units across sessions and build an overall UMS total. That rewards consistent unit performance and clear “cash-in” planning. On **Cambridge**, more of the risk sits in the final paper set for that series — so past-paper timing and full-paper stamina matter more.

If you switch boards mid-course, map topics carefully: Pure content overlaps heavily, but **paper style and mark wording do not**. Practising the wrong board’s papers trains the wrong reflexes.

## How marking differs in practice

Both boards award credit for valid method and correct answers, but the published schemes use different conventions and phrasing. That is why board-specific marking beats generic AI grades.

- Edexcel IAL Maths: open [/mark?board=edexcel](/mark?board=edexcel) or the [IAL Mathematics hub](/edexcel/international-a-level/mathematics) (units WMA11–WST02).

## Where OxfordAQA fits

[OxfordAQA International](/oxfordaqa) is a third international option — linear A-levels with a growing footprint and thinner free tooling online. MarkScheme has subject shells for Maths, Physics, Chemistry and Biology; marking for OxfordAQA waits until Edexcel IAL Maths conversion proves the multi-board funnel.

## Practical pick

1. Take the board your school teaches well.  
2. Confirm university recognition for your target country/course.  
3. Revise **that** board’s papers and mark schemes — not a mix.  
4. Use marking that speaks the board’s dialect: Cambridge schemes or Edexcel IAL method/accuracy for Maths.

## Bottom line

Cambridge and Edexcel IAL are comparable in standing and different in structure. Choose for teaching quality and assessment shape, then practise the real paper style. MarkScheme’s job is the second half: examiner-style feedback once you have written an answer.
